# Behavioral Signals

`Home › 04-trust-calibration › Behavioral Signals`

Trust calibration is behavioral, so measure it that way, not by self-report.

| Signal | What it indicates |
|---|---|
| Over-reliance | Approvals granted regardless of evidence quality; errors passing through unchallenged |
| Under-use | The AI's output is generated but the human silently redoes the work anyway — no benefit realized |
| Rubber-stamping | Fast, uniform approval times are a useful proxy — approvals that don't vary with case complexity suggest the evidence isn't actually being reviewed |
| Automation bias | A tendency to accept AI output specifically because it's AI-generated, independent of its actual quality |
| Workload migrated into exception handling | The volume "eliminated" by automation reappears as exception review load — track whether this is genuinely a reduction or a relocation |

## Why this connects to the autonomy ladder

These signals are exactly what [promotion-and-demotion-criteria.md](../03-progressive-autonomy/promotion-and-demotion-criteria.md) uses to decide whether a workflow is ready to move up a rung, or needs to move down one. Rubber-stamping detected at the assist stage is a strong argument against promoting to approve-to-act — the human layer isn't actually exercising the oversight the current rung assumes.
