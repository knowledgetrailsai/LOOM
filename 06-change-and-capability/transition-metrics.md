# Transition Metrics

`Home › 06-change-and-capability › Transition Metrics`

Measure the transition itself, not just the eventual outcome — these are early signals of whether a workflow is landing, well before the [outcome metrics](https://github.com/knowledgetrailsai/OASIS/blob/main/methodology/chapter-18-evaluation-and-reliability-engineering.md) move.

| Metric | What it signals |
|---|---|
| Time-to-proficiency | How long it takes users to become effective with the redesigned workflow |
| Correct use | Whether the workflow is actually being used as designed, not worked around |
| Override rates | Feeds directly into the [autonomy ladder](../03-progressive-autonomy/promotion-and-demotion-criteria.md)'s promotion/demotion criteria |
| Workaround behavior | Users routing around the system entirely is a stronger signal of a broken design than a low satisfaction score |
| Users' sense of control | A proxy for whether authority was actually designed clearly, or only documented as if it was |

## Why these come before outcome metrics

Outcome metrics (Chapter 18, Chapter 26) tell you whether the business result improved, but they move slowly and can be confounded by many other factors. Transition metrics move fast and are directly attributable to the workflow redesign itself — a rising override rate or workaround pattern in week two is actionable long before a quarterly outcome metric would show anything.
